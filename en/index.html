Practice

Exercises for developing intuition

Practical techniques grounded in neuroscience research. Do them on your own or as preparation for the seminars.

All exercises are based on research in cognitive neuroscience and psychophysiology. The difficulty level is shown by the color of the left edge: ■ beginner · ■ intermediate · ■ advanced. Start with the beginner level and move step by step.

🎧
“The Body Compass”
■ Beginner

Developing interoception: the ability to feel and recognize body signals, the first carriers of intuitive information.

  1. Sit comfortably and close your eyes. Take 3 slow breaths.
  2. Think of a decision you're unsure about. Just hold the thought, don't analyze it.
  3. Scan your body: where does a sensation appear? Warmth, tension, lightness, heaviness?
  4. Write down exactly where it is and what it feels like, without interpreting it.
⏱ 10–15 minutes
Garfinkel & Critchley, 2016
⏸
“The Pause”
■ Beginner

A technique for interrupting the automatic stress response. It helps you keep access to your inner resource in a difficult situation.

  1. When you feel stress building up, name it out loud or silently: “I'm stressed right now.”
  2. Make your exhale longer than your inhale (4 seconds in, 7 out).
  3. Ask yourself: “What do I know about this situation?” Not “what's happening”, but “what do I know”.
  4. Wait 90 seconds. The neurobiology: that's how long the initial chemical stress reaction lasts.
⏱ 5 minutes
Taylor, 2009 · My Stroke of Insight
🔄
“The Distance”
■ Intermediate

A cognitive distancing technique for telling fear, desire and a clean intuitive signal apart.

  1. Imagine yourself 5 years from now. You're looking at this situation from a distance in time.
  2. From there: which decision feels right? What would this future version of you advise?
  3. Come back to the present. Compare the answer with what your body feels right now.
  4. If both signals match, it's probably clean intuition. If they differ, that's a signal to analyze.
⏱ 15–20 minutes
Ozlem Ayduk, Ethan Kross, 2010
🌊
“Letting the Problem Go”
■ Intermediate

A technique for sparking insight by deliberately shifting attention. The unconscious keeps working, it just needs space.

  1. Dive deep into the problem: gather all the information there is. Give your brain a full “download”.
  2. Deliberately stop thinking about it. Switch to something monotonous (a walk, washing the dishes).
  3. Keep a notebook nearby: the insight can come at any moment.
  4. When the thought appears, write it down right away, without judging it.
⏱ 20–40 minutes
Dijksterhuis, 2006 · Science
🎯
“The Bias Filter”
■ Advanced

A method for spotting the cognitive biases that distort an intuitive signal. It takes honesty with yourself.

  1. Put into words the decision that “feels” right. Write it down.
  2. Ask: “Do I want this decision?” If yes, check whether it's desire dressed up as intuition.
  3. Ask: “Am I afraid of the alternative?” If yes, check whether it's avoidance rather than wisdom.
  4. If the answer to both is “no”, the signal is cleaner. Gather 2–3 independent opinions to confirm it.
⏱ 25–30 minutes
Kahneman & Klein, 2009 · Psych. Rev.
🔮
“Simulating the Future”
■ Advanced

The pre-mortem: imagining the “failure” before you decide. It activates prospective memory and reveals hidden risks.

  1. Imagine: decision A has already been made. Six months have passed, and everything went wrong. What happened?
  2. Describe the failure scenario as concretely as you can. Write down the causes.
  3. Repeat for decision B and for “do nothing”.
  4. Compare. Which decision led to the least catastrophic “failure”? Often that's the wisest choice.
⏱ 30–45 minutes
Klein, 2007 · Harvard Business Review

About

Beyond the Horizon

A science-based, hands-on program for developing intuition and decision-making skills under uncertainty. In Ukrainian it's called «Крок за горизонт», “a step beyond the horizon”.

Beyond the Horizon grew out of the meeting point of neuroscience, cognitive psychology and hands-on experience working with people in real uncertainty. Our central idea: intuition is not a gift, it's a skill.

We build on the research of Daniel Kahneman (Nobel Prize, 2002), Gary Klein (naturalistic decision-making), Antonio Damasio (somatic markers) and current work in neurophysiology and interoception.

Over the years, more than 2,000 participants from different cities and professions have taken the seminars and courses. From doctors and entrepreneurs to teachers and creative professionals.

The format keeps evolving: we record video lectures, publish seminar recordings and develop new exercises. This site is a living archive and a resource for everyone on the way.

The program's scientific basis

Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, Fast and Slow. - the System 1 and 2 model
Klein, G. (1998). Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions. MIT Press.
Damasio, A. (1994). Descartes' Error. - the role of emotions and the body in decisions
Garfinkel & Critchley (2016). Interoception, contemplation and health. Frontiers in Psychology.
Soon et al. (2008). Unconscious determinants of free decisions. Nature Neuroscience.
